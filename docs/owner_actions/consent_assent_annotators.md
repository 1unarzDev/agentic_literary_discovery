# Annotator information and consent (for every annotator; under-18s need a parent/guardian signature)

**Project:** "What Stands in the Way": a student research project measuring how obstacles in English fiction changed between 1800 and 1929, using AI-assisted reading checked by people.

**What you would do:** read short passages from old, public-domain novels (about 350 words each) and record a few judgements in a spreadsheet: is something standing in a character's way, what kind of thing, and where does it come from. About 30 seconds per passage; sessions of 20–30 minutes; total time is listed in your packet.

**Voluntary:** taking part is your choice. You can stop at any time without giving a reason, and you can ask for your labels to be removed before the results are analysed (around Nov 9).

**Privacy:** your labels are stored under an anonymous ID (e.g. `peer1`). Your name is not published or attached to the data. Only aggregate results are reported.

**Content:** passages come from 19th- and early-20th-century fiction and may contain outdated or offensive language or themes typical of that period. If a passage upsets you, skip it (answer `cant_tell` with a note).

**No payment, no risk beyond ordinary reading.** Acknowledgement in the paper is offered if you would like it (optional, by name or anonymously).

**Questions:** ask the student who invited you.
