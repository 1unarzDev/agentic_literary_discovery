## Decisions for the paper

1. **Claim a narrower construct.** The strongest contribution is a validated distinction between a domain's presence and its function as an impediment to a character's goal, tracked by domain and material/social/internal locus. Cite Häußler–Gius for historical conflict, Conroy/Radak/Palkó for inwardness, and Kim–Pethe–Skiena for night-time history.
2. **Keep H1 falsifiable.** Internal obstacle share is distinct from mental-verb frequency, FID and embodied agency. Compare absolute rates as well as conditional shares: a falling material denominator can raise the internal share without an absolute internal increase.
3. **Treat H2 directions as project hypotheses.** The critical works justify studying technology and narrative form, not presuming that infrastructure simply removes plot trouble. Railways can create accidents or confinement; postal systems create interception and secrecy; illumination can support new night activity.
4. **Make H3 an empirical comparison.** Prespecify which mention and function series are compared, with uncertainty and period-sensitive annotation checks. Prior warnings about proxies do not establish that this corpus's keyword and function trends diverge.
5. **Use correction methods as foundations.** DSL/PPI can improve downstream estimation given an appropriate gold-label sampling design. They cannot repair an invalid construct or turn publication-date associations into modernization's causal effects.
6. **Position the benchmark against D5, LSC-Eval and POPPER.** Specify what is known under each null (e.g., stable obstacle function despite changing genre mixture or lexical cues), which claims are unsupported, and when an agent should abstain. Novelty lies in this literary evaluation target, rather than synthetic evaluation itself.

## Final novelty verdict

**No verified work located in this targeted sweep already measures the proposed multi-domain, goal-blocking obstacles and material/social/internal locus across approximately 15,000 UK/US English novels over 1800–1925.** That combination is a plausible contribution, conditional on annotation validity and evidence. It is not proof of worldwide priority: full native Scholar/Semantic Scholar coverage and several paywalled full texts remain **UNVERIFIED**.

**Historical computational conflict measurement already exists**, including 217 German novels in Häußler and Gius (2023) and diachronic relational arcs in Christou and Tsoumakas (2025). Computational inward-turn tests and historical night-time measurement also already exist. Therefore avoid “first computational history of conflict,” “first quantified test of the inward turn,” or “first measurement of lighting-era change in fiction.” The railway n-gram chapter especially warrants full-text follow-up before a strong H2b priority claim. The bibliography establishes precedents and motivation; it does not verify the project's hypothesized historical results or the causal wording “modernity rewrote.”
