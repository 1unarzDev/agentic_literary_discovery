# Diffusion sources and audit

Checked 2026-10-31. **Decision:** CHAT supplies useful annual context, but its `mail` is not a letters-only series and UK telephone counts have an administrative break. Use diffusion as descriptive exposure, not evidence that an obstacle disappeared or a causal instrument. Missing observations are not zero. Dates below describe observed nonblank cells, not invented coverage.

## CHAT: exact files, columns, coverage

Verified the requested [GitHub repository](https://github.com/datasets/historical-adoption-of-technology), its [schema](https://github.com/datasets/historical-adoption-of-technology/blob/main/datapackage.json), [raw CSV](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/datasets/historical-adoption-of-technology/main/data/historical-adoption-of-technology.csv), and [retrieval script](https://github.com/datasets/historical-adoption-of-technology/blob/main/scripts/process.py). The script identifies the original NBER CSV. [NBER's dataset landing page](https://www.nber.org/research/data/cross-country-historical-adoption-technology) links the [original appendix directory](https://data.nber.org/data-appendix/w15319/), containing `FinalCHAT_72909.csv`, `chat.dta`, and `README.TXT`. [Comin and Hobijn's original accompanying paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15319/w15319.pdf), especially its variable-definition table, supplies meanings and underlying sources. This is one source lineage, not independent replication.

Keys: `country_name`, `year`; country strings are exactly `United Kingdom` and `United States`. Requested columns exist as `mail`, `telegram`, `telephone`, `railline` (there is no column named `railway`). Related columns: `railp`, `railpkm`, `railt`, `railtkm`. GitHub metadata declares `year` a date, but actual cells contain integer year strings. No imputation was performed.

**Units and comparability require care.** The paper defines mail as items sent/received, counting internal items once and cross-border items for each country, with varying inclusion of newspapers, registered mail and parcels. For these UK/US cells, mail is expressed in millions: US 1900 `7130` corresponds to USPS's 7,129,990,000, rounded; UK 1925 `6060` matches the broad 1925–26 packet total below. Telegram values likewise appear in millions (e.g. US 1910 `75`, against Western Union's 75.135 million). This scaling is verified by spot checks, not by an explicit unit field in the GitHub schema; **do not assume the same scaling for every technology/country**. The paper's sources for mail, telegram and railway are Mitchell's historical-statistics volumes.

The paper defines `telephone` as year-end mainline connections to the public switched network, not telephone subscribers, households, calls, or instruments. UK values rise from 123,000 (1910) to 707,000 (1911); 1900 is only 2,700. Treat pre-takeover counts as **UNVERIFIED national totals**. Post Office-only coverage is a plausible explanation, but the precise CHAT/Mitchell table note was not verified. The [1911 Post Office estimates debate](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1911/may/18/post-office) distinguishes Post Office and company systems and anticipates the National Telephone Company takeover on 1 January 1912. Thus a financial-year label can also differ from the calendar event year.

`railline` means route length open at year end, generally including narrow gauge but excluding mountain, purely industrial and urban systems; it is not track length counting parallel tracks. [DataHub's current description](https://datahub.io/technology/historical-adoption-of-technology) specifies kilometres. Divide by 1.609344 for miles and label any conversion. Country labels alone do not establish historical territorial coverage: check Great Britain versus UK/Ireland in the underlying source. Related rail units also need verification before pooling: `railp` US 1900=596 versus UK=665123 shows that a blanket scaling rule would be unsafe.

## Postal series: what is actually available

**UK letters, annual 1839–1925:** a complete, consistently defined letters-only machine-readable series was **UNVERIFIED** in this search. CHAT is the practical broad-mail series, with four wartime gaps, but should not be renamed “letters delivered.” [Hansard, Letter Packets, 24 November 1930](https://hansard.parliament.uk/%E2%80%8CCommons/1930-11-24/debates/58afec7f-963e-4156-ace3-fd075242253e/LetterPackets) gives decennial packet totals; these are not letters alone. [Hansard, 24 May 1927](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1927/may/24/statistics) reports 5,783 million letters/postcards/printed-paper/newspaper packets in 1913–14 versus 6,060 million in 1925–26, and explicitly changes territory from Great Britain and Ireland to Great Britain and Northern Ireland. [1926 workload answers](https://hansard.parliament.uk/%E2%80%8CCommons/1926-04-27/debates/e55fd1a9-bd1a-41d1-89e7-d22143e6c000/WrittenAnswers) separate item classes and note the boundary change from 1922–23.

A conspicuous baseline difference: [London Museum](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/penny-post-sparked-letter-writing-revolution/) says about 76m letters in 1839 and 169m in 1840; CHAT has 82m and 169m. [Hemmeon's historical account](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/42983/42983-h/42983-h.htm) explicitly includes franks in its 82m-plus 1839 figure. This is a definition issue to resolve before plotting one continuous letters-only curve. Digitizing the Postmaster General's annual reports, retaining fiscal-year and item-class labels, is the next step for that target.

**US mail:** [USPS, Pieces of Mail Handled … Since 1789](https://about.usps.com/who/profile/history/pieces-of-mail-since-1789.htm) provides an isolated 1847 count, annual 1886–1913, isolated 1923, then annual counts from 1926. Blank cells are explicitly unavailable, despite annual post-office and finance columns. This corroborates the principal CHAT gaps; CHAT lacks the 1847 observation. [USPS First-Class Mail Volume Since 1926](https://about.usps.com/who/profile/history/first-class-mail-since-1926.htm) is outside the study period and should not be projected backward; detailed annual class estimates start in 1926. These URLs were readable through web search/open, although direct HTTP requests returned 404 in this environment.

These national aggregates describe infrastructure availability imperfectly; they do not establish access for a fictional character, a rural setting, or a novel's historical story time.
